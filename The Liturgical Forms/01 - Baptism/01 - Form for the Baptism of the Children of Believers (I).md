# Form for the Baptism of the Children of Believers (I)

Brothers and sisters, as we come to the baptism of Full Name there are a few things worth thinking about regarding the sacrament of baptism.

Why do we baptise?

We baptise because baptism is a mark of discipleship and the sign of God's covenant of grace.

After Jesus rose from the dead, but before He ascended to heaven, He commissioned His followers with this task: "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you".[^1]

Throughout history God has marked His people with a special sign. God first made a covenant with Abraham, saying: "I will establish My covenant as an everlasting covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you for the generations to come, to be your God and the God of your descendants after you".[^2] The sign of that covenant was circumcision.[^3]

When Jesus came, God made a new covenant with His people with a new sign to replace circumcision. The sign of this new covenant was baptism. In the same way as circumcision marked someone as being part of the covenant, so too baptism marks a person as being part of the renewed covenant in Jesus Christ.

Baptism marks a person as a recipient of the covenant promise but also marks them as a disciple; someone who is learning to follow and obey Jesus.[^4] On the one hand, baptism is about the initiating, gracious promises of God, before anything we say or do. On the other hand, baptism is about an entry into the covenant community that follows the Lord of the covenant — Jesus Christ.

As a result, a baptised child is not left to follow Jesus on their own. God calls a people, not just individuals, to be His treasured possession.[^5] This means a baptised child has the benefit of not only growing up in a Christian family that has the responsibility of discipling them, but also a church family that will spur them on in the faith. So, when we baptise someone in our church, we initiate them into the church and we make a commitment to disciple them; helping them to know and follow Jesus Christ.

Why do we baptise with water?

We baptise with water because it is a sign of washing and renewal. Just as water cleanses our bodies, so the blood of Christ cleanses us from our sins.[^6] Baptism is a physical sign, which is applied once, to point us to the spiritual cleansing of Jesus' sacrifice and the renewing work of the Holy Spirit[^7] — a work which is both complete and ongoing.

Why do we baptise in the name of the Father, Son & Holy Spirit?

We baptise in the name of the Father, Son & Holy Spirit because each person of the Trinity gives us certain promises in baptism. God the Father will adopt us as His children and heirs.[^8] God the Son will cover our sins with His perfect sacrifice.[^9] And God the Holy Spirit will dwell in us, helping us to obey, uniting us to the church, and sealing in us the promise of glory.[^10] By being baptised into the singular "name" of God, we state that God is unifying us as "one body", just as the Father, Son and Holy Spirit are One.[^11] This unity among us is shown outwardly by our shared baptism.[^12]

Why do we baptise children of believers?

We baptise the children of believers because they are, by God's grace, included in the covenant promise. As Peter says at Pentecost, after calling for repentance and baptism: "The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off — for all whom the Lord our God will call."[^13]

So, the children of believers, too, receive the sign of the covenant and discipleship because through their believing parents and the church community, they will learn about the great hope of this covenant: salvation through Jesus Christ.

We baptise the children of believers because the Bible says they are considered "holy" through their believing parents (or parent).[^14] This means, they are set apart, along with God's holy church, "to declare the praises of Him who called us out of darkness and into His wonderful light."[^15]

We don't baptise the children of believers because they have a personal faith — they may not be able to articulate whether they have that yet or not. Similarly, we don't baptise them because the parents' faith is somehow transferred to the child. And we don't baptise them because they are guaranteed to become Christians — rejection is a possibility, just as it is with baptised adults, and just as it was for those who were circumcised in Israel. No, we baptise children of believers because the sign of the new covenant belongs to them.[^16]

So, we who follow Jesus must not hinder our children from coming to Him, but must disciple them; leading them, instructing them, and modelling to them that forgiveness and grace is found only in Jesus Christ.

Baptism therefore places the child, the parents and the church under certain obligations. Name of child has a future obligation to accept, by faith, the washing away of their sins which Jesus has accomplished on the cross and to submit to Him as Lord. Name of Parents have an obligation to disciple the child as they grow to know the Lord Jesus. And the church has an obligation to partner with the parents to see this discipleship process through.

Baptism is a gracious sign of God's initiating grace in salvation and an outward representation that this child has a place among us as his/her church.

Now, having explained what baptism is and why this child should be baptised, we will ask the parents and the church to answer the following questions.

Question to Parents:

Names of Parents , as you have now heard, baptism is given to us by God because He made a covenant with us and our children. We baptise our children because it is a sacrament that Jesus Himself instituted for His covenant people. As parents, you are asked to answer the following questions:

1. Do you confess Jesus Christ as your Lord and Saviour, accept the promises of God, and affirm the truth of the Christian faith which is taught in the Bible and confessed in this church of Christ?

2. Do you believe that your child, though sinful by nature, is received by God in Christ as a member of His covenant and His church, and therefore ought to be baptised?

3. Do you promise, in reliance on the Holy Spirit and with the help of the Christian community, to do all in your power to instruct this child in the truth of the Christian faith and to lead him/her by your example to confess and follow Jesus Christ as their own Lord and Saviour?

Answer: By the grace of God, we do.

Question to the congregation (optionally have the congregation stand):

Do you, fellow disciples of Jesus Christ, promise to receive Full Name   in love? Will you pray for and help instruct him/her in the faith? Do you promise to model the Lord Jesus for him/her as a congregation, so that that he/she may grow up knowing and loving God, being surrounded by Christian examples?

Answer: By the grace of God, we do.

Baptism:

In administering baptism, the minister shall say:

Full Name , I baptise you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.

(Prayer of thanksgiving can be offered here).
