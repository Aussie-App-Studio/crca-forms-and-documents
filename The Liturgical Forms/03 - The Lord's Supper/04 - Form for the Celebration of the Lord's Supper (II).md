# Form For The Celebration
Of The Lord's Supper (II)

EXHORTATION

Beloved in the Lord Jesus Christ,

In the night before He went to the cross,

"...Jesus took bread, gave thanks and broke it, and gave it to His disciples, saying, 'Take and eat, this is My body.'

Then He took the cup, gave thanks and offered it to them saying, 'Drink from it, all of you. This is My blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins. I tell you, I will not drink of this fruit of the vine from now on until that day when I drink it anew with you in My Father's kingdom'."[^1]

God's Word commands all believers to proclaim Christ's death at this communion.[^2] Until He comes again, we are to remember Him under the signs of bread and wine, which represent His body crucified, and His blood poured out, for our sins.

Thus this Supper first of all tells us that Jesus as the Good Shepherd laid down His life for the sheep.[^3]

"...He was pierced for our transgressions,

He was crushed for our iniquities;

the punishment that brought us peace was upon Him..."[^4]

At this table, we, with the hand and mouth of faith, through the mysterious work of the Holy Spirit, truly receive Christ who gave Himself on earth in the sacrifice of the cross, and now sits at the right hand of God the Father Almighty.[^5]

This Supper also speaks to us of the fellowship we have with one another. It is one table around which we meet, one bread that we eat,[^6]and one wine which we drink. The Holy Spirit who unites us with our exalted Saviour, also binds us together in brotherly love. Thus, around the Lord's table we give visible expression to that Body of which He is the Head and we are the members.[^7]

And so, living from forgiving grace, we extend forgiveness to one another.[^8] As He served us, we are each other's servants.[^9] As He loved us first, we love one another.[^10] As He intercedes for us, we pray for one another.[^11]

Finally, the celebration of the Lord's Supper makes us look ahead with deep longing towards the Day of the Lord's coming again. In the night before His death He said that He would not eat the Passover again with His followers "until it finds fulfilment in the kingdom of God."[^12] Joyfully we confess that the promised kingdom has begun to come with power: in the glory of Christ's resurrection, His ascension into heaven, and the outpouring of the Holy Spirit. It is in that kingdom that our exalted Lord already has His supper with His Church on earth.

Yet, as we struggle to make His royal claims known to a rebelling world, we eagerly await that final fulfilment when He will appear before all eyes and hand over the kingdom — completed! — to God the Father, so that God may be all in all.[^13] Thus, we celebrate the wedding feast of the Lamb here on earth as a foretaste and assurance of the perfect joy to come. "Blessed are those who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb!"[^14]

CONFESSION OF SIN

The congregation may be invited to join in.

As we see the abundant grace which the Lord shows us in this Supper, it becomes even clearer to us how far we have drifted away from our God and how grievously we have broken His holy commandments, provoking His anger.

We therefore do not come to this Supper to demonstrate how perfect we are in ourselves. We are straying sheep and faithless children. Therefore we cry to God: "Do not bring Your servant into judgment!"[^15]

ASSURANCE OF PARDON

But thanks be to God, who in boundless mercy offers forgiveness to all who sincerely repent and seek their refuge in Christ Jesus.

"For God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son, that whoever believes in Him shall not perish but have eternal life."[^16]

"Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life, but whoever rejects the Son will not see life, for God's wrath remains on him."[^17]

In the suffering and death of Christ, not only the terrible weight of sin becomes manifest, but also the limitless extent of God's love:

"For His anger lasts only a moment, but His favour lasts a lifetime!"[^18]

All who trust in Jesus Christ, the Crucified and Risen, are accepted by God in grace. They may share at this table the communion with the Lord Jesus Christ and with all who in trial and persecution look with uplifted head towards the great Day of the Lord.

However, all who trust in themselves for their peace with God, and those who do not repent of their sins, those who do not desire humbly to live in obedience to God and in peace with their neighbour, are urgently called to seek the newness of life which God's grace in Christ can give. Otherwise they should not come to this table lest they eat and drink judgment to themselves.[^19]

Let us pray.

It is our duty and delight, Lord God our Father, to give You thanks and praise for all You have done for the world. You called it into being that it should be to the praise of Your glory in everlasting life and beauty, led by man made in Your image.

Then, when Your covenant partner fell and became Your enemy, leading all Your handiwork astray into death, You sent Your Son in sovereign love to redeem him and his world.

We thank You that Jesus was born among us to be the Second Adam; that He lived our common life in this valley of tears Your world had become; that He suffered our sorrows and the God-forsaken agony of hell; that He died our death.

But, Father, we praise You that death could not hold Him and that He now lives, ever to make intercession for us at Your right hand, yet is present with us through the Holy Spirit. We pray that the power of that Spirit may so use this Holy Supper that we may truly celebrate our union with Jesus Himself, rejoicing in His victory and strengthening each other in the hope which You have brought to this earth of Yours.

We thank You with all Your saints in heaven and on earth, for this Gift which is greater than words can express, our Lord Jesus Christ.

Amen.

The congregation is requested to arise. The minister indicates which of the two creeds to use, and says:

Let us now unite with the Church of all ages in the glad profession of the Faith once delivered to the saints, of which we speak with heart and mouth, saying:

EITHER:

APOSTLES' CREED

I believe in God the Father Almighty,

Maker of heaven and earth.

And in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.

Who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,

born of the virgin Mary;

suffered under Pontius Pilate,

was crucified, dead, and buried;

He descended into hell.*[^20]

The third day He rose again from the dead;

He ascended into heaven,

and is seated at the right hand of God the Father Almighty;

from there He shall come to judge the living and the dead.

I believe in the Holy Spirit,

the holy catholic*[^21] Church,

the communion of saints;

the forgiveness of sins,

the resurrection of the body,

and the life everlasting.

Amen.

OR:

NICENE CREED

We believe in one God,

the Father almighty,

maker of heaven and earth,

of all things visible and invisible.

And in one Lord Jesus Christ,

the only Son of God,

begotten from the Father before all ages,

God from God,

Light from Light,

true God from true God,

begotten, not made;

of the same essence as the Father.

Through Him all things were made.

For us and for our salvation

He came down from heaven;

He became incarnate by the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary,

and was made human.

He was crucified for us under Pontius Pilate;

He suffered and was buried.

The third day He rose again, according to the Scriptures.

He ascended to heaven

and is seated at the right hand of the Father.

He will come again with glory

to judge the living and the dead.

His kingdom will never end.

And we believe in the Holy Spirit,

the Lord, the giver of life.

He proceeds from the Father and the Son,

and with the Father and the Son is worshipped and glorified.

He spoke through the prophets.

We believe in one holy catholic*[^22] and apostolic church.

We affirm one baptism for the forgiveness of sins.

We look forward to the resurrection of the dead,

and to life in the world to come.

Amen.

The congregation is seated.

The minister then issues the invitation to communicant members and guests known to Session and says:

Jesus Christ welcomes to the fellowship of this table all those who are sincerely sorry for their sins and renew their promise to live a godly life. So come then with glad assurance and joyful praise.

A psalm or hymn of praise is sung.

In congregations where the people come forward to the table, they now do so. Then the minister says:

That we, then, may be nourished with Christ, the true Bread from heaven, let us lift our hearts to heaven, where He is, our Mediator, at the right hand of the Father. We must not doubt that we shall receive His body and blood as our food unto life eternal, through the gracious work of the Holy Spirit, as truly as we receive this bread and drink in remembrance of Him.

In breaking and distributing the bread, the minister shall say:

The bread which we break is the sharing[^23] of the body of Christ. Take it, eat, remember and believe that the body of our Lord Jesus Christ was given for a complete forgiveness[^24] of all our sins.

In serving the cup, the minister shall say:

The cup of thanksgiving[^25] or which we give thanks, is the sharing[^26] of the blood of Christ. Take it, drink from it, all of you, remember and believe that the precious blood of our Lord Jesus Christ was poured out[^27] for a complete forgiveness[^28] of all our sins.

While the elements are being distributed, or for a brief period following that, the minister or another member of the congregation may read fitting passages from Scripture, or a psalm or hymn may be sung.

Suggested readings for the thanksgiving at the conclusion of the day's celebration of the Lord's Supper:

Psalm 36:5-10.

Psalm 103:1-4; Psalm 103:8-13; Psalm 103:20-22.

Romans 5:8-10; Romans 8:31-39.

Revelation 4:11; Revelation 5:13.

Prayer of thanksgiving:

Heavenly Father, God of all mercy,

You have set our feet on the pathway of life, through the sacrifice unto death of Jesus Christ, Your Son. When we were at our end where only hell awaited us, You made a new beginning through Him who bore our condemnation.

Make us now, we pray, Your messengers of peace: willing to forgive, ready to sacrifice, patient and persistent as we extend to others Your invitation to a new and holy life.

Give us the vision to see that the whole world belongs to You, and the courage to resist the false claims of the evil one. Make the message of Your great salvation reach both those who have never heard it and those who have strayed away.

Lord, we look for the Day to come soon, when we shall see our Saviour no longer in the signs of broken bread and poured-out wine, but in glory coming on the clouds of heaven, to unite Your whole creation in everlasting peace and praise.

Through Him, our Lord Jesus Christ, who taught us to pray:

Our Father in heaven,

hallowed be Your name,

Your Kingdom come,

Your will be done

on earth as in heaven.

Give us today our daily bread.

Forgive us our sins,

as we forgive those

who sin against us.

Lead us not into temptation

but deliver us from the evil one.

For the kingdom, the power and the glory

are Yours,

now and for ever.

Amen.

The intercessions of the congregation for both local and universal needs may also be incorporated in this last prayer.

In smaller congregations, this prayer of praise and intercession around the table of the Lord could be led by more than one person at the direction of the Session.
