# Form For The Celebration
Of The Lord's Supper (III)

Beloved in our Lord Jesus Christ,

In this service our Lord calls us to His table to have communion with Him as well as with each other. Let us listen to what the gospel tells us about the first celebration of the Lord's Supper:

In the night before He went to the cross,

"Jesus took bread, gave thanks and broke it, and gave it to His disciples, saying, 'Take and eat, this is My body.'

Then He took the cup, gave thanks and offered it to them, saying, 'Drink from it, all of you. This is My blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins. I tell you, I will not drink of this fruit of the vine from now on until that day when I drink it anew with you in My Father's kingdom'."[^1]

It is the will of the Lord that all believers join in this communion. For although it is true that our sins make us unfit to come into His presence, it is just as true that Christ offered Himself as a payment for all our sins. Surely, this is what the Supper teaches us. All who trust in Jesus Christ, the Crucified and Risen, are accepted by God in grace. They may share at this table the communion with the Lord Jesus Christ and with all who, in trial and persecution, look with uplifted head towards the great Day of the Lord.

However, all who trust in themselves for their peace with God, and those who do not repent of their sins, those who do not desire humbly to live in obedience to God and in peace with their neighbour, are urgently called to seek the newness of life which God's grace can give. Otherwise they should not come to this table lest they eat and drink judgment to themselves.[^2]

The bread and wine represent the body and blood of the Saviour. They are received in faith as signs and seals of all the benefits of His sacrifice on the cross.

The bread and wine are an illustration and guarantee to believers of the forgiveness of sins, of the strengthening of our faith, and of the communion of believers with the Lord as well as with each other.

These signs and seals of the covenant of grace declare that God is faithful in fulfilling the promises of that covenant, they call upon us to live as God's children in this world and they encourage us to look forward with joy to the Day when God will be all in all.

Let us pray:

The congregation may be invited to join in.

Merciful God, we do not come to this table trusting in our own goodness and virtue. We come because Christ has invited us. We come because we are sinful people in need of forgiveness. We come because we are hungry for life and need to be fed.

Father, forgive us and feed us. We come in wonder and gratitude to offer our very selves to You in worship and adoration.

Father, accept our praise, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

The minister then issues an invitation to communicant members and guests known to Session and says:

Jesus Christ welcomes to the fellowship of His table all those who are sincerely sorry for their sins and who renew their promise to live a godly life. So come then with glad assurance and joyful praise.

A psalm or hymn is sung.

In congregations where the people come forward to the table, they now do so.

The minister says:

Lift up your hearts! Look to your Lord in faith!

In breaking and distributing the bread, the minister shall say:

The bread which we break is the sharing[^3] of the body of Christ. Take it, eat, remember and believe that the body of our Lord Jesus Christ was given for a complete forgiveness[^4] of all our sins.

In serving the cup, the minister shall say:

The cup of thanksgiving[^5] for which we give thanks, is the sharing[^6] of the blood of Christ. Take it, drink from it, all of you, remember and believe that the precious blood of our Lord Jesus Christ was poured out[^7] for a complete forgiveness[^8] of all our sins.

While the elements are being distributed, or for a brief period following that, the minister may read fitting passages from Scripture, or a psalm or hymn may be sung.

Suggested readings for the thanksgiving at the conclusion of the day's celebration of the Lord's Supper:

Psalm 36:5-10.

Psalm 103:1-4; Psalm 103:8-13; Psalm 103:20-22.

Romans 5:8-10; Romans 8:31-39.

Revelation 4:11; Revelation 5:13.

Prayer of thanksgiving.

The congregation may be invited to join in.

EITHER:

For the bread that we have eaten,

for the wine that we have tasted,

for the life that You have given,

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit,

we will praise You!

For the life of Christ within us,

turning all our fears to freedom

helping us to live for others:

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit,

we will praise You!

For the strength of Christ to lead us

in our living and our dying,

in the end with all Your people:

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit,

we will praise You!

Amen.

OR:

Father of our Lord Jesus Christ,

we bring You glory, thanks and praise,

for all that we have seen and heard,

tasted and handled, of the Word of Life;

for the fellowship with You

and with Your Son,

and with all Your people, too.

We humbly ask You, to accept us

as we dedicate ourselves to You — again.

Help us with Your daily grace,

that we may continue in this fellowship,

that our hearts may be filled

with new courage and hope;

that we may live to Your glory.

Through Jesus Christ, our Lord. Amen.
