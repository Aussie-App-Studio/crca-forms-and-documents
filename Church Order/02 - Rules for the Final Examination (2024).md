# Rules for the Final Examination of Candidates for the Ministry of the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia

*Appendix: Examination Rules — Adopted at Synod 2024*

## General

The purpose of this examination shall be to ascertain whether the candidate is able to pursue the work of the ministry of the Word and sacraments in the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia to the edification of the people of God and the progress of the church. Classis needs to ensure that it can heartily recommend the candidate for ordination. The Classis shall not conduct this examination in such a way that it is a mere repetition of the college examination. This examination is not an open book exam and is not supposed to be of an academic but ecclesiastical nature. The churches must know whether the candidate is sound in doctrine and is able to apply his knowledge in a suitable manner.

## A. Requirements

1. A statement from the Reformed Theological College (RTC) – or equivalent institution indicating that the Candidate has successfully completed the regular, prescribed study in theology, and testifying to his Christian conduct while a student.
2. A statement from the Session (or Church Council) to which the Candidate belonged during his candidature or training, regarding his doctrine and conduct.
3. A statement from a medical doctor about the Candidate’s physical fitness.
4. A statement from classical deputies who have discussed with the Candidate the motives causing him to seek the ministry.

## B. Examination guidelines for Specimen Sermon

1. The final exam is to be held in the Classis, where the candidate is completing his vicariate or training.
2. The Classis will ensure that two Synodical Deputies from two separate Classis outside the examining Classis be present. These deputies will have a voice and a vote, but not the right of veto.
3. The exam will follow the following process:
   - **a.** The Candidate will be asked to provide a specimen sermon with exegetical notes on a passage prescribed to him by the appointed sermon examiners. The candidate shall forward copies of the specimen sermon to the Classis Clerk at least one month prior to the examination for distribution to the examiners and to the sessions. This sermon will be examined and must meet the standard required to be sustained.
   - **b.** The Candidate will also provide two further sermons with exegetical notes, of his own choice, and these sermons must also meet the standard to sustain an exam. Each of these two sermons should address a different genre of Scripture; either Old Testament History, Wisdom, Prophecy or Psalms; or New Testament Gospel, Parable, Epistles or Revelation.
   - **c.** The classical examiners shall evaluate the content of these sermons on the basis of the guidelines adopted by Synod 1985 Art 82.2:
     - **i.** Accuracy of exegesis.
     - **ii.** Does the outline carry the text?
     - **iii.** Is the context, including the historical redemptive, taken into account?
     - **iv.** Is the basic thrust Christ-centred?
     - **v.** Is the sermon doctrinally sound?
     - **vi.** Is the teaching applied in a manner consistent with the text?
     - **vii.** Is the theme developed in a coherent logical manner?
   - **d.** The classis examiners shall give reasons at the examination why the Classis should either sustain or not sustain the sermon. Classis shall decide on the recommendations of the examiners before the candidate preaches the sermon.
   - **e.** Where the Classis decides to sustain the sermon, the examination continues and the candidate shall preach the sermon. The Classis shall judge if the candidate shows gifts of communication and the deliver is edifying, taking into account the experience of the candidate.
   - **f.** A two-thirds majority is required to sustain the sermon part of the exam.
   - **g.** In the event that the Classis decides for serious reasons not to sustain the sermon, the candidate shall not be asked to preach the sermon but continue with the oral section of the examination. If the Classis sustains the oral examination, then the candidate shall, before the next meeting of the Classis, prepare another sermon on a text chosen by himself and forward copies of this sermon and brief exegetical notes to the Classis Clerk, who will distribute it to the examiners and to all the Sessions at least one month prior to the next Classis meeting. Classis at this meeting shall hear the evaluation of the examiners and decide whether or not to sustain the sermon. If sustained, the Classis proceeds with 3e with the exception, of course, of the remainder of the examination.

## C. Examination guidelines for Oral examination

1. Exegesis: The examination in the exegesis of both Old and New Testaments shall cover one or two passages from one chapter in each Testament. The assignment of the two chapters shall be made at least three weeks prior to the examination. The reading of the Bible in the original languages and a lengthy discussion of introductory problems shall be omitted. The Classis shall satisfy itself that the candidate is able to expound the Scriptures. The candidate shall be examined in each Testament for 15 minutes.
2. Dogmatics: The manner in which a candidate is to be examined must be of a practical rather than of an academic nature. The purpose is not to repeat the final college examination. The churches may leave this to the RTC or equivalent institution. The churches have to assure themselves that the candidate is sound in doctrine and is capable of giving a clear exposition of the Reformed doctrines and of defending the Reformed position over against heresies which are popular in our time, in order that as shepherd of Christ’s flock he may be able to protect that flock and “hold firmly to the trustworthy message as it has been taught, so that he can encourage others by sound doctrine and refute those who oppose it” (Tit 1:9). The candidate shall be examined for at least 30 minutes in this subject.
3. The oral sections for the examination include the following:
   - **i.** Old Testament & New Testament Exegesis (15 Minutes each),
   - **ii.** Dogmatics, Ethics & Symbolics (45 Minutes),
   - **iii.** Church History (15 Minutes),
   - **iv.** Church polity (10 Minutes)
   - **v.** Knowledge of Scripture (10 Minutes)

## D. After the Examination

1. On completion of the sermon and oral sections of the examination, Classis requires a two-third majority or more to sustain the exam.
2. After the successful completion of the examination, the Candidate shall subscribe to the Confessional Standards of the Christian Reformed Churches of Australia in the presence of the Classis.
3. The Classis shall give the Candidate a statement which allows him to be ordained as a minister of Word and sacraments.
4. After the successful completion of the examination, the Candidate may receive calls immediately but cannot accept a call to serve a calling church until six weeks have expired (As per Synod Decision 1965 Art 30.1).
